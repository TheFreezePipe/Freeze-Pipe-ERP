## 1. Picking: queue/claim, batch & tote start, next-pick card, confirmation sequence, qty handling, exceptions, completion

### Takeaway
The dominant pattern is: choose/accept a batch, declare tote count (or scan a cart), then a repeating loop of "go to location -> scan item (and sometimes location first) -> place into the tote that belongs to that order -> scan tote to confirm", with a Report Issue / three-dot menu for exceptions. Vendors differ mainly in (a) whether a location scan is mandatory (ShipHero: optional per account; Logiwa, Hopstack, SKUSavvy, Odoo: location first), (b) whether multi-qty is scan-per-unit or typed (ShipHero can force scan-per-unit; Logiwa/Odoo allow either), and (c) what an exception does to the order (ShipHero marks the location "Hospital" and may drop the order from the batch; Logiwa branches on backorder/partial-ship settings; Veeqo and Hopstack documentation is silent).

### Inferences
- [inference] Consensus loop for a custom phone picker: one primary instruction per screen (location -> item -> qty -> tote), with the tote/cell identity as the largest object after location, and a visible counter (picked/required) that the scanner itself drives. Odoo's per-order color bar and ShipHero/Logiwa cell/tote coloring show color is used to bind a line to an order, not to signal status.
- [inference] The exception surface everyone converges on is a three-dot/"Report Issue" menu rather than a primary button, with a reason list (missing, damaged, label unreadable, skip). Logiwa's "Skip and Pick Last" and ShipHero's "Hospital" location are the two clearest models of non-blocking handling; Logiwa's matrix of backorder x partial-ship settings is the clearest model of what happens to the order.
- [inference] "Scan tote again to confirm" (ShipHero, Logiwa Pick and Sort) is a deliberate mis-drop guard; the video text says the app "directs each unit to the correct tote", i.e. the tote is chosen by the system, not the picker.
- [inference] ShipHero's per-user "Enforce All Pick Scans" and Cin7's "force bin scan"/"allow manual quantity" are configurable strictness switches; a custom app should treat scan-per-unit and location-scan as per-user or per-station policy rather than hard-coded.

## 2. Packing: pack-station screen, verification, box, weight, label, mismatches, multi-box, slips, desktop vs tablet

### Takeaway
Pack stations are scan-driven two-pane screens: entry via tote/order/cart scan (or a list picker), an "items to pack" list that moves scanned lines to a "packed" list, then package type, weight, carrier/service and a single "Complete & Print" action that is only enabled once prerequisites are met. Mismatch is handled by an immediate error (plus optional sound) rather than a dialog flow; over-scan is a warning. ShipHero and Logiwa both support "no touch" command barcodes for the completion step.

### Cited Findings
- ShipHero Packing App (desktop/USB scanner): entry "Single Orders" page in List View (filtered by packer defaults) or Scan Mode (toggle upper right) where scanning a tote opens the order (faster than list; scan required for MIB). Order shows a list side and a **Packed** side; with Tote QA on, each item must be scanned and moves to Packed; with Tote QA off all picked items move automatically; **Unpack** returns an item. Wrong barcode: error (plus error sound if "Sound Feedback" enabled for the user); scanning an item already at required qty: warning to prevent over-packing. Box via dropdown, scan of box barcode, or automation rule; dimensions and weight auto-fill; weight updates as items packed or from a connected scale. **Add Another Package** for multi-box. Optional Parcel View barcode takes a photo of box contents. Print options "Print invoice & label", "Print invoice", "Print label"; finish by scanning/selecting the **Complete Order** barcode, then the app returns to the main screen. Order attachments appear as a pill at top. ## 7. General UX guidelines for scanner-driven warehouse UIs (credible sources)

### Takeaway
Credible, citable numeric guidance exists for touch size (NN/g 1 cm; Google 48dp with 8dp spacing; Zebra 60dp standard / 48dp floor; WCAG 2.2 AA 24 CSS px minimum), contrast (WCAG 4.5:1 text / 3:1 large), and response-time feedback (NN/g 0.1/1/10 s). Warehouse-specific human-factors material (Lucas/Voxware/Honeywell) is mostly vendor-authored; Zebra publishes feedback-channel options but no explicit rulebook for scan confirmation. Glove-specific target-size numbers were not found from a primary source.

### Cited Findings
1. **Touch targets, general:** NN/g: interactive elements at least 1 cm x 1 cm physical; ~2 mm minimum spacing is insufficient for tiny targets; primary CTAs larger (Target's main search and scan buttons ~2 cm x 2 cm); users who are walking need bigger targets; limit element count so key targets can be larger. — [NN/g touch target size](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/touch-target-size/)
2. **Touch targets, Android:** at least 48 x 48 dp (about 9 mm), separated by 8 dp or more; hit area may extend beyond the visible icon. — [Google Accessibility Help: touch target size](https://support.google.com/accessibility/android/answer/7101858)
3. **Touch targets, rugged devices (Zebra EC30 guide, which tells devs to build on Material Design):** min touch zone 4.7 mm / 6 mm recommended (citing UXmatters); standard buttons 60 dp; 80 dp near screen edges; hard floor 48 dp; 8 dp grid; padding 10-16 dp; body text 14 pt, captions 12 pt, nothing under 12 pt; UXmatters hand-holding stats: ~75% use one thumb to tap, <50% hold in one hand, ~36% two hands, ~10% cradle+tap; tap accuracy varies by screen location (center preferred). — [Zebra EMDK EC30 Programmer's Guide](https://techdocs.zebra.com/emdk-for-android/15-1/guide/ec30_programming)
4. **WCAG 2.2 AA target size:** pointer targets at least 24 x 24 CSS px, with spacing/equivalent/inline exceptions; a stricter 44 px level exists as 2.5.5 (AAA). — [W3C Understanding SC 2.5.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html)
5. **Contrast:** WCAG 1.4.3: 4.5:1 for normal text, 3:1 for large text (>= 18 pt, or 14 pt bold). — [W3C Understanding SC 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html). Warehouse-UI article advice: use color sparingly for status, large text for location/item/qty, accessible contrast helps gloved/low-light use [vendor/3rd-party] — [Racklify on warehouse UI](https://racklify.com/encyclopedia/designing-warehouse-software-user-interfaces-practical-guidance/)
6. **Feedback latency:** NN/g: ~0.1 s feels instantaneous (no extra feedback beyond showing the result); about 1 s is the limit for uninterrupted thought flow and a "working" signal is needed beyond that; 10 s requires a percent-done indicator and a way to interrupt. — [NN/g response times](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/response-times-3-important-limits/)
7. **Scan feedback channels:** Zebra SDK spec: developer chooses LED, beeper and vibration indicators for success; noisy retail may use beeper+LED, hospitals vibration+LED; red/green LEDs can signal in/out of stock. — [Zebra SDK Android spec sheet](https://devc-downloads.zebra.com/content/dam/zebra_dam/en/spec-sheets/sdkandroid-spec-sheet-en-us.pdf). Utrecht master's thesis: enhanced sound design reduced perceived workload and annoyance vs conventional tones in WMS scanning; Siena paper: auditory error feedback can expose worker mistakes publicly, suggests haptic+visual at workstations. [academic, thesis-level, small studies; via search summary] — [Utrecht thesis](https://studenttheses.uu.nl/handle/20.500.12932/46062), [Siena paper](https://usiena-air.unisi.it/bitstream/11365/1262274/3/paper_hs.pdf)
8. **Per-task scan profiles and decoding:** Honeywell-focused guidance (third-party Cleverence) recommends native scan APIs/intents over keyboard wedge, per-task scan profiles (presentation mode at pack bench vs trigger in picking), picklist/center mode to avoid misreading adjacent labels on dense labels. [3rd-party] — [Cleverence Honeywell guide](https://www.cleverence.com/amp/articles/technical-manuals/scanner-settings-on-honeywell-ck65/)
9. **Camera scanning:** Google ML Kit: use 1280x720 or 1920x1080 (not native resolution), smallest barcode element >= 2 px, wait for the same result across consecutive frames in live video, prompt user to move closer/zoom when barcode too far. — [ML Kit barcode scanning (Android)](https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/vision/barcode-scanning/android)
10. **Voice vs RF human factors:** Lucas Systems (vendor) argues voice keeps eyes and hands free vs RF handheld, claims 5-10% productivity from removing the handheld and larger gains combined with optimization; presents hybrid voice + back-of-hand scanning. Vendor-reported, not independent. — [Lucas: voice vs RF](https://www.lucasware.com/voice-picking-compared-to-rf/), [Lucas voice selection guide](https://cdn.deposco.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Lucas-Voice-picking-Selection-Guide.pdf)
11. **Glove-friendly hardware:** Zebra WT6000/WT6300 reseller pages say displays work with gloves and when wet and adjust sensitivity; no numeric target size for gloves. [3rd-party reseller] — [Levata WT6000](https://www.levata.com/zebra-wt60a0-tx0lewr-weareabls-terminal)

### Inferences
- [inference] A conservative default for Freeze Pipe's phone picker: primary buttons >= 60 dp (about 11 mm) tall, nothing interactive under 48 dp, 8-16 dp gaps, body >= 16 px and location/qty text much larger; primary action bottom-anchored (one thumb; Zebra/UXmatters stats), secondary exceptions behind a three-dot or "Report issue" button.
- [inference] Scan acknowledgement should land within ~100 ms (visual + haptic) to feel instantaneous; any lookup over 1 s needs a spinner; avoid sound-only error signals (Siena privacy point, noise); pair color with icon/text.
- [inference] Gloved use: no authoritative number found; staying in Zebra's 60-80 dp bands is the most defensible proxy.
