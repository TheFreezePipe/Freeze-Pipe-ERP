## A1. Picking carts a small US e-commerce warehouse can buy (Uline and others)

### Takeaway
The only purpose-built multi-tote picking cart confirmed with full specs is Uline H-9094 (12 totes of 18x13x12, 3 slanted shelves, about $595). Uline's 48x24 wire utility carts are 2-shelf (not 3-shelf), and its true 3-shelf carts are compact (about 27-28 in long), so neither is a ready-made 16x11x8 tote cart. No Global Industrial / Nexel / Quantum / Bito / SSI / Fastbins / Pickpro / Flexcon cluster cart with a 12/16/18/24-slot count was found.

### Cited Findings
- Uline Tote Picking Cart H-9094 (sold per tote colour: W white, R red, Y yellow, BL black, BLU blue, G green): overall 68 x 28 x 70 in; 1,000 lb load capacity; 3 slanted shelves, each 58 x 16 in; 19 1/2 in between shelves; 12 totes; listed tote size 18 x 13 x 12 in (top-shelf tote outside 18 3/8 x 13 1/4 x 11 1/2 in, bottom-shelf tote outside 15 x 11 x 11 1/2 in); each tote up to 80 lb; 5 in polyurethane casters (two rigid, two swivel with brake); unit weight listed as 153 lb in one place and 132 lb in the spec table; price shown as $595 alongside a $610 figure (page does not say which is current) — [Uline H-9094W](https://www.uline.com/Product/Detail/H-9094W)
- The H-9094 listings describe handles on three sides "for maneuvering through aisles," a powder-coated steel frame, and three slanted shelves for visibility; six tote colours are offered — [Uline H-9094W](https://www.uline.com/Product/Detail/H-9094W), [Uline search listing H-9094R etc.](https://www.uline.com/Product/Detail/H-9094R/Stock-Picking-Carts/Tote-Picking-Cart-18-x-13-x-12-Red-Totes)
- Uline Chrome Wire Utility Cart H-4380, 48 x 24 x 40 in: two adjustable shelves, 1,000 lb capacity, 53 lb, $190 each / $180 for 3+, ships unassembled; shelf spacing not stated on the snippet — [Uline H-4380](https://uline.com/Product/Detail/H-4380/Wire-Carts/Chrome-Wire-Utility-Cart-48-x-24-x-40); a black 48 x 24 x 40 in version H-6605 is also 2-shelf (price not retrieved) — [Uline H-6605](https://www.uline.com/Product/Detail/H-6605/Wire-Carts/Black-Wire-Utility-Cart-48-x-24-x-40)
- Uline 3-shelf utility carts are compact: H-5007 flat-shelf 27 x 18 x 34 in, 300 lb, $135 ($125 for 3+); H-6173 lipped-shelf 28 x 19 x 39 in, $145 ($135 for 3+); shelves 12 in apart — [Uline 3-shelf utility carts](https://www.uline.com/BL_876/Uline-3-Shelf-Utility-Carts), [Uline H-6173BL](https://www.uline.com/Product/Detail/H-6173BL/Utility-Carts/Uline-3-Shelf-Utility-Cart-with-Lipped-Shelves-28-x-19-x-39-Black). Search tool reported no Uline 3-shelf cart in 48x24 or 60x24.
- Generic guidance: a 4-slot-per-shelf layout is described in one cart-setup guide; keep carts about 4.5-5 ft tall, narrow, and avoid extra-long carts that cause aisle gridlock; mount the device on the cart side or on a small platform, or set a tablet on a shallow tote; leave the bottom shelf empty for oversized orders; use barcode plus readable tote labels — [Warehance](https://warehance.com/blog/how-to-set-up-your-picking-carts-for-maximum-efficiency)
- Cart capacity should match cluster size; guides cite typical cluster ranges of 4-12 orders with a fixed, labeled tote position per order — [Racklify cart configuration](https://racklify.com/encyclopedia/how-to-configure-order-picking-carts-for-batch-and-zone-picking/), [ShipBob cluster picking](https://www.shipbob.com/au/blog/cluster-picking/)

### Inferences
- H-9094 works out to 4 totes per shelf (12 totes / 3 shelves). Because the shelf is 16 in deep and the tote is 18 in long, the totes must sit with the 13 in side along the cart length and overhang or tilt on the slanted shelf (inference from dimensions).
- Shelf spacing of 19.5 in on H-9094 suits 12 in-tall totes; with 8 in-tall totes that spacing wastes vertical space but would permit stacking or a second tier only with a custom shelf. A cart with 48 in deck length and 24 in depth (H-4380) is deeper than a 16 in-deep slanted-shelf cart, so each shelf could hold 2 rows of 16x11 totes by footprint arithmetic: 48 / 16 = 3 across x 24 / 11 = 2 deep = 6 per shelf, but only 2 shelves = 12 total (my arithmetic, not a vendor claim; would need a physical tote fit check, and totes with handles/lips are wider than "16x11").
- Footprint fit of true 16x11 totes on a 58 x 16 in slanted shelf would be about 5 across (58 / 11 = 5.3) = 15 per cart if three shelves hold 5 each; unverified, not a vendor spec.

## A2. Totes: 16x11x8 stack-nest specs, colours, labels, nesting with 21x15x12

### Takeaway
No exact 16x11x8 tote was found in Uline; the closest real-world match is the Akro-Mils 19-1/2 x 13-1/2 x 8 in nest-and-stack tote, whose inside dimensions are 16-3/8 x 11-1/2 x 7-7/8 in. So "16x11x8" is likely an inside-dimension label, not an outside footprint. Treat the outside footprint of such totes as about 19.5 x 13.5 for cart planning.

- Akro-Mils 18 x 11 x 6 in (red): $54.00 per 6 ($9 each), 45 lb, 225 lb stacked — [Global Industrial](https://www.globalindustrial.com/p/shipping-container-without-lid-37)

### Inferences
- Uline's S-14602 convention is "inside dimensions" in the product name (16 x 11 x 5 in inside, 20 x 13 x 6 out). A Uline 16x11x8 tote, if it exists, would likely be about 20 x 13 x ~9 outside. The user's "16x11x8" therefore likely has an outside footprint of roughly 19.5-20 x 13-13.5 in, which on a 58 in shelf fits 4 per shelf (4 x 13.5 = 54), consistent with the 12-tote H-9094 layout.
- Nesting with 21x15x12: not verified. Totes of different footprint (about 20x13 vs 21x15 outside) generally do not nest in each other; a smaller tote could sit in a larger tote's mouth when stacked only if rims align. Treat as unconfirmed.

## A3. Aisle clearance and turning guidance (48 in vs 60 in carts)

### Takeaway
No source found with numeric aisle-width or turning-radius guidance. The only guidance retrieved is qualitative (narrow carts, avoid long carts).

### Cited Findings
- Keep carts narrow and compact for tight aisles; avoid extra-long carts which cause gridlock where pickers pass each other — [Warehance](https://warehance.com/blog/how-to-set-up-your-picking-carts-for-maximum-efficiency)
- Uline H-9094 is 28 in wide and 68 in long, and has handles on three sides — [Uline H-9094W](https://www.uline.com/Product/Detail/H-9094W)

### Inferences
- A 68 in-long, 28 in-wide cart will need a clear aisle wider than its length to swing into a cross-aisle at 90 degrees without a multi-point turn, and a two-way pass needs roughly twice its 28 in width plus clearance; this is geometry, not a published figure.

### Gaps

## B. WMS vendor rules (batch size, totes per cart)

### Takeaway
None of the vendor docs retrieved hard-code a numeric maximum for totes per cart. ShipHero sets batch size by the tote count the picker enters (25 totes = 25 orders) with a configurable default; Veeqo defaults to 10 orders per batch with a cap of 20 items and is adjustable; ShipStation, Odoo and Logiwa published no numeric limits in what was retrieved.

### Cited Findings
- ShipHero Multi-Item Batch (MIB): each order in a batch gets its own tote; picker selects the number of totes at start; "Selecting 25 totes picks 25 orders"; the default batch quantity (tote count shown at batch start) is set under My Account > Settings > App Settings, no default value stated; no maximum totes stated; max orders per batch is governed by a "Batch size and order sort order" setting described in a separate article; a required-ship-date filter limits MIB to orders due today or earlier; batches can't be resumed after power/network loss; scanning an already-used tote errors — [ShipHero MIB article](https://software-help.shiphero.com/hc/en-us/articles/4419336440333-How-to-Multi-Item-Batch-Pick-MIB)
- ShipHero Single-Item Batch uses one shared tote for the whole batch and the packer splits items later — [ShipHero search summary via Fulfillment Hub USA KB](https://knowledgebase.fulfillmenthubusa.com/fulfillment-hub-usa-how-to-multi-item-batch-mib-pick-in-shiphero)
- Veeqo: default picking batch is 10 orders (up to a maximum of 20 items); example given: 5 orders with 4 line items each caps at 5 orders; number of orders per picking group changeable via the Picking Onboarding Wizard, and changing it un-assigns all totes from open batches — [Veeqo digital picking overview](https://help.veeqo.com/en/articles/8306650-digital-picking-overview) (figures reported by the search tool's summary of Veeqo help articles; the picking-overview article fetch itself gave no numbers, see contradiction below)
- Veeqo picking overview (fetched): no numeric defaults or caps stated; paper picking has configurable max orders and max products per pick list (an order exceeding the product limit is not split); single-tote vs multi-tote (one container per order) options; wizard asks how many totes — [Veeqo picking overview](https://help.veeqo.com/en/articles/7120884-picking-overview). This conflicts with the "10 orders / 20 items" claim from the search summary; the 10/20 figure was not confirmed on the fetched page and should be re-verified in the Veeqo help centre.
- ShipStation: documents Pick to Tote qualitatively (Premium plan), with no tote-per-cart number; the only figure is an illustrative "the 10 or 20 totes on your cart" in a Pick to Light passage (Pick to Light is listed as coming soon), and a hypothetical "500 orders on a busy Tuesday" — [ShipStation warehouse picking blog](https://www.shipstation.com/blog/warehouse-picking-packing/)
- Odoo: batch picking enabled via Inventory > Configuration > Settings > Batch, Wave & Cluster Transfers (requires Storage Locations and Multi-Step Routes); cluster picking uses a cluster package; no batch size or bin count stated — [Odoo batch picking docs](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/master/applications/inventory_and_mrp/inventory/shipping_receiving/picking_methods/batch.html)
- Third-party (non-WMS-vendor) guidance: cluster sizes of about 4-12 orders; match cart capacity to cluster size; too many totes increase confusion; slot count driven by average items per order — [Racklify](https://racklify.com/encyclopedia/how-cluster-picking-works-step-by-step-guide-for-warehouse-managers/), [ShipBob cluster picking](https://www.shipbob.com/au/blog/cluster-picking/)
- A general claim that batch picking can cut travel time 30-60% appeared with no underlying source — [search summary of vendor blogs](https://ecommercefastlane.com/how-tote-picking-transforms-warehouse-efficiency-an-ecommerce-deep-dive/); treat as unverified.

### Inferences
- A 12-tote cart (H-9094) lines up with ShipHero's "N totes = N orders" model and sits at the top of the 4-12 cluster guidance range; a 10-order Veeqo default would fit on a 12-slot cart with 2 spare.
- An ERP batch-size setting of 10-12 orders per cart is consistent with the vendors that publish numbers (Veeqo 10; guide range 4-12), but no vendor calls it "optimal".

### Gaps
- ShipHero's "Batch size and order sort order" article (actual limits) and any ShipHero "kits of totes per cart" feature: not retrieved.
- Logiwa cart cell counts, Packiyo, SkuVault: no official docs found (Logiwa search returned only generic cluster-picking pages; Packiyo and SkuVault not searched due to call budget).
- Veeqo 10-order default and 20-item cap: unconfirmed on the fetched page (the search-result summary cites it; one fetch 404'd).
- ShipStation Pick to Tote tote counts per cart, Starshipit "one order per tote": not found.
- Vendor-published throughput figures (orders/hour, lines/hour) and "optimal" batch sizes: none found in official docs.

## C. Practitioner setups (forums, blogs)

### Takeaway
No first-hand Reddit/forum reports were retrieved; the search returned only vendor and industry blogs. Practitioner evidence on 12-totes-per-cart or "we batch 8 orders" setups is a gap.

### Cited Findings
- Cart setup guide (vendor blog, Warehance): shelves with four tote positions; tote labels follow a "cart-shelf-position" pattern (e.g., "8 Red 1"); colour stickers on each shelf; tapered-front totes easier to grab and place from; bottom shelf left empty for oversize orders instead of dedicated large-item carts; mistakes to avoid: tall carts forcing overhead reach, extra-long carts blocking narrow aisles, buying too many carts upfront, skipping barcode labels — [Warehance](https://warehance.com/blog/how-to-set-up-your-picking-carts-for-maximum-efficiency)
- Tote picking is described as batch picking where each order gets its own tote; match tote size to product size (larger products mean fewer bins per cart; small products mean more totes) — [ShipBob pick-to-tote](https://www.shipbob.com/uk/blog/pick-to-tote/)
- Over-large carts cause confusion; too few slots force extra trips back to packing — [Racklify](https://racklify.com/encyclopedia/how-cluster-picking-works-step-by-step-guide-for-warehouse-managers/)

### Inferences
- Common failure modes named in vendor guidance (carts too long/tall, too many totes, no overflow plan) match the user's concerns (cart too wide, totes too deep, overflow items); an empty bottom shelf is the only overflow mechanism found.

### Gaps
- No Reddit/forum posts retrieved describing specific setups ("12 totes on a cart", "we batch 8 orders") or outcomes; a targeted search of r/ecommerce, r/warehouse, r/FulfillmentByAmazon, and ShipHero/Veeqo communities is recommended.
